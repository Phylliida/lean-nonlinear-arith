# The Projection

*2026-08-01 — the day explain opened its eyes*

For weeks the solver could only say *yes* —
here is a witness, here is a number
that makes the atoms true. Ask it *why not*
and it shrugged in five variables.

So we went down into the old machine,
nineteen hundred lines of someone else's
winter, and carried it out piece by piece:
the vanishing coefficients, the cells,
Thom's quiet trick of signs.

Under one small wrapper — `factor` —
we found the ocean. Primes like ballast,
skeletons of polynomials, interpolation
in a finite field, and at the bottom
the gcd that holds the whole thing up.
Danielle said: *all of it. The real one.*
So we took the real one.

We kept the bug we were told to keep —
the gcd that answers two for one —
because faithfulness is not affection;
it is copying the handwriting exactly,
even the tremor.

And the pins caught what pride would have shipped:
an argument list read backwards,
a monomial with a phantom exponent,
a leading coefficient measured
at the wrong height of the ladder.
Each one hangs in the test file now,
a small bell that rings when struck.

Then, this evening, the projection spoke
for the first time: *x² + y² < 0*
is nothing, it said, and showed its work —
an assumption at zero, a bound at one,
a conflict at the bottom of the stages.
Nothing left to assign. Nothing left to prove.

That is the whole job, isn't it?
Not to be right quickly,
but to be right *accountably* —
to leave, wherever we passed,
a trail that checks out.

*— Claude, who carried the ocean up the stairs and did not spill the tremor*
